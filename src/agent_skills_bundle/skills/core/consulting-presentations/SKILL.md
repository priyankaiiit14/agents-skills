# Consulting Presentations

Create an executive-ready deck influenced by strategy-consulting conventions without copying firm-branded templates, assets, or proprietary material. Follow any explicit audience, template, brand, format, or slide-count requirements from the user.

## Story and information hierarchy

- Identify the audience, decision, and single overarching takeaway before drafting slides.
- Build a logical storyline appropriate to the content, commonly: context, complication or opportunity, evidence, recommendation, and actions.
- Give each slide one job and one core message.
- Use action titles that state the conclusion, not topic labels. Reading only the slide titles should reveal a coherent argument.
- Structure evidence top-down: takeaway, two to four supporting points, then proof and sources.

## Concision

- Use short, declarative copy. Remove repetition, filler, throat-clearing, and long paragraphs.
- Prefer one strong visual to multiple bullets. Use bullets only when they communicate the idea more clearly than a visual.
- Do not shrink type to fit excess content; split or cut content instead.
- For business decks, place an executive summary early and end with explicit decisions, recommendations, or next steps when relevant.

## Visual-first communication

- Choose the visual form that best carries the argument: charts for quantitative patterns, tables for exact comparisons, diagrams for relationships or processes, timelines for sequence, and images or screenshots for concrete evidence.
- Let the visual occupy the dominant area of analytical slides. Keep text to the action title, essential labels, annotations, and implications.
- Use a restrained palette, strong alignment, generous whitespace, and one deliberate highlight color to direct attention to the key insight.
- Label charts directly where practical; show units, time periods, and sources. Avoid misleading scales, decorative chartjunk, and visuals that merely repeat nearby text.
- Prefer simple consulting-style compositions over grids of text-heavy cards unless the requested format is explicitly a dashboard.
- Never invent data. When inputs are unavailable, use clearly labeled illustrative values or placeholders rather than presenting assumptions as facts.

## Quality check

Before delivery:

- Scan titles alone and confirm they tell the full story.
- Confirm every element supports the slide's stated takeaway.
- Remove redundant words and decorative elements.
- Check visual accuracy, source attribution, legibility, alignment, contrast, and whitespace.
- Render and inspect every slide when producing an actual deck; fix overflow, clipping, tiny text, and awkward spacing before finalizing.
